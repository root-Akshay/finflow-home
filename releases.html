Release notes for FinFlow on Android

FinFlow is distributed directly rather than through the Play Store, so this page is where changes get written down. The download link always points at the newest build.

24 September 2026 · v1.1.0

Backup & restore

Everything FinFlow knows lives in a single database on your phone. Nowhere else, by design — but that also meant an uninstall, a lost phone, or a bad update took it all with it, permanently. That gap is now closed.

Under Profile → Data → Backup & restore, FinFlow can write its entire database to a file you keep, and read one back. The backup holds not just your transactions but everything built on top of them: the merchants it has learned, the ones you have merged or renamed, your spending patterns and categories. Restoring returns you to exactly where you were.

It is worth doing once now, while you do not need it.

Two things to know when restoring. FinFlow will ask you to turn notification access off first — it reads your bank alerts in the background through a second connection to the same database, and replacing the file underneath that can corrupt it. And when it says to force-close the app afterwards, do that before opening anything else. Both prompts are load-bearing, not formalities.

Also in this build

  • Fixed a date calculation that silently reported nothing instead of your history span.
  • Corrected internal timestamp handling that could date records to 1970.

Worth knowing

  • A backup is a plain, unencrypted database file. It holds your full financial history — keep it somewhere you would keep a bank statement, and think twice before putting it in a shared folder.
  • Your app settings — notification tone and toggles, card appearance, layout preferences — are not part of the backup. After restoring you will need to set those again. Your financial data is unaffected.
  • Installing this over an existing FinFlow keeps your data. No uninstall needed.
23 September 2026 · v1.1.0

First public build

The first version of FinFlow available to anyone outside of testing.

FinFlow reads the alerts your bank already sends to your phone and turns them into a transaction history you never have to type in. It recognises merchants, sorts spending into categories, learns your recurring payments, and tells you what it notices — in sentences rather than charts.

Your transactions stay on your device. Signing in and the optional Friends and Shared Stories features use Firebase; the spending data itself does not leave your phone. The privacy policy sets out exactly what is stored where.

Known gaps

  • AI narrative insights are inactive in public builds. FinFlow falls back to its own local, rules-based summaries — you still get observations, just not AI-written ones.
  • Requires Android 7.0 or newer.
